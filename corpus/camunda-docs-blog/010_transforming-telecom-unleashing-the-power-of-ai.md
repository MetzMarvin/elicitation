## What the page shows

The post describes where AI can be inserted into telecom service provisioning, and the
captured figure is the process model it annotates: start event "Request Service" into the
collapsed "KYC" sub-process, then "AI Select Packages", "AI: Rules for Additional Combined
Packages" and a third options step, the gateway "Service Confirmed?", the task "Confirm
Service Availability", the gateway "Service Available?", the task "Assign Resource", the
collapsed "Provide Services" sub-process, the gateway "Additional Services Requested?" with
a "Repeat as necessary" loop back to "Assign Resource", and on to "Notify Customer" and
"Bill Customer" - with a "No" branch from "Service Available?" to a second "Notify Customer"
task - ending at "Services Provided". The page refers to the figure directly when it says of
inventory checks: "This is not indicated in the image above but could be handled in the
subprocess around Provide Services ."

The AI surface is marked in two ways. Two tasks are named for it - "AI Select Packages" and
"AI: Rules for Additional Combined Packages" - and bracketed annotations attach AI to
otherwise ordinary tasks: "Using DMN and/or AI, determine appropriate resource for service
request" on "Assign Resource", "AI to help provide invoice data" under "Bill Customer", and
the customer notification e-mails described as "(generated by AI)". The page's narrative AI
example is stated for a different telecom process, tariff filing: "The filing process can
invoke AI agents that can take the rate and line changes as input to locate all the affected
tariffs and even note the changes that need to be made to assist the editor when making
tariff modifications."

## Observations (description only, no interpretation)

- **AI activity - function:** the page's stated function is agent-assisted location of
  affected tariffs - "The filing process can invoke AI agents that can take the rate and line
  changes as input to locate all the affected tariffs and even note the changes that need to
  be made to assist the editor when making tariff modifications"; in the captured model the
  annotated functions are package selection and upselling options ("AI can review the
  services and offer options for combinations or upselling"), resource assignment, invoice
  data and generating the notification e-mail.
- **AI activity - element type:** a mixture of a dedicated naming convention (tasks labelled
  "AI Select Packages", "AI: Rules for Additional Combined Packages") and annotations that
  bind AI to ordinary tasks ("Using DMN and/or AI, determine appropriate resource for service
  request"); no separate AI node type, connector icon or element-template binding is visible.
- **Authority - downstream:** the captured model's chain from the AI-labelled tasks - into
  the gateway "Service Confirmed?", then "Confirm Service Availability", the gateway "Service
  Available?", "Assign Resource", the "Provide Services" sub-process, then "Notify Customer"
  and "Bill Customer" (or the rejection branch to the second "Notify Customer"), reaching
  "Services Provided".
- **Authority - data:** the page names the agent's inputs for the filing process only - "the
  rate and line changes"; the captured figure prints no variables or data objects, and the
  closest thing to a data statement is the annotation "AI to help provide invoice data".
- **Authority - control:** the three exclusive gateways with X markers ("Service Confirmed?",
  "Service Available?", "Additional Services Requested?") and the annotated human-facing
  options step, whose note reads "A human or Bots can be automated to discuss AI-generated
  options with the prospect".
- **Input provenance:** for the figure's process, the customer request form ("Customer fills
  out request form to start process", annotation on the start event); for the page's
  tariff-filing example, "the rate and line changes".
- **Guards present:** not stated as such on the page - the nearest thing is the annotation
  that the AI-generated options are discussed with the prospect by "A human or Bots"; no
  review or confirmation gate on the AI output is drawn in the captured figure.
- **Prompt / model detail visible:** not visible on the page - no model name, prompt text or
  connector parameter appears in the brief; "Using DMN and/or AI" is the only implementation
  hint, and it is stated as an either/or.

## Notes for the researcher

Two things to weigh. First, a provenance point: the ruling's AI sentence ("The filing
process can invoke AI agents ...") describes the page's tariff-filing example, while the
captured figure is the service-provisioning model - the second figure on the page, alt
"Process model showing opportunities for AI integration" - so the quote and the artefact come
from two different sections of the same post. The page has a third figure, alt "Assistive AI
helps review changes", which is the closer illustration for the filing section and was not
captured. Second, a legibility point: the source asset is only 623x225 and the w=1400
download is an upscale (1400x506 on disk), so the element labels are blurred; the AI-labelled
tasks and the annotations quoted above are readable, but the label of the options task
between "AI: Rules for Additional Combined Packages" and the "Service Confirmed?" gateway
could not be read with certainty (it appears to read "Present Customer with Options"), so it
is not named in this record.
