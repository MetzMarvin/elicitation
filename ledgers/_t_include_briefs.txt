  Defining the elements of a business process model
  First, break down the components of a process model, such as activities, events, gateways, and flows.
  Activities: These are the fundamental tasks or units of work within the process. Visually represented as rectangles or rounded rectangles in business process modeling notation (BPMN) , activities can be as straightforward as sending an email or as complex as completing a customer onboarding process.
  Events: Events are points in the process that signify when something happens, such as the start, an intermediate occurrence, or the end of a process. They are depicted as circles in BPMN and can significantly influence the flow of the process.
  Gateways: Serving as decision points within a process, gateways determine branching and merging paths, guiding the process flow based on certain conditions or rules. They are represented as diamonds and help manage the complexity of operational decisions.
  Make sure to describe the role of participants/actors, resources, inputs, and outputs in a model.
  The clarity and effectiveness of a business process model hinge on the use of a standardized modeling notation, such as BPMN. This standardized language provides an array of graphical elements that are universally recognized, allowing for a consistent and precise representation of business processes
  BPMN, in particular, is designed to be understandable by all business stakeholders, creating a collaborative bridge between technical and nontechnical users. Its significance lies in its ability to foster clear communication, eliminate ambiguities, and provide a solid foundation for process analysis
  This is the cornerstone of an effective business process model—a clear, comprehensive representation of how a business functions, enabling continuous optimization and alignment with strategic goals.
  Not all business process models are created equal (although you can use the same tool for all of them). Different types of models serve various purposes, from conveying the big picture to enabling precise operational changes.
